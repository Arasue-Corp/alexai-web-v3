First-time renter?
A checklist for getting started.

The Alex AI Team
• June 25, 2025 • 5 min read

Renting your first apartment is an exciting milestone, but it can also feel overwhelming. From budgeting to signing the lease, there are many steps to navigate.

This checklist will guide you through the process to ensure a smooth and successful start to your rental journey.

Phase 1: The preparation

Before you even start looking at listings, get your finances and paperwork in order to move quickly when you find "the one."

  • Set a budget: A common rule is the 30% rule—aim to spend no more than 30% of your gross monthly income on rent. Don't forget utilities and internet.
  • Check your credit score: Landlords will almost always run a credit check. Know your score beforehand and be prepared to explain any blemishes.
  • Gather your documents: You'll likely need proof of income (pay stubs, offer letter), references, and a government-issued ID.

Phase 2: The hunt

Now for the fun part. As you tour apartments, look beyond the fresh paint and keep these critical factors in mind:

  • Ask the right questions: What utilities are included? What is the pet policy? How is maintenance handled (24/7 or only business hours)?
  • Document everything: Take photos and videos of each unit you visit. Pay close attention to any existing damage so you aren't blamed later.

"Reading the lease carefully is the single most important step. Don't be afraid to ask for clarification on any clause you don't understand before you sign."

Phase 3: The final steps

You've found the perfect place! Here is how to lock it down securely.

  • Review the lease agreement: Read every single word. Understand the terms regarding the security deposit, late fees, and guest policies.
  • Get renters insurance: This is a non-negotiable step. Your landlord's insurance covers the building, but not your personal belongings. It protects you from theft, fire, and liability.
  • Do a final walk-through: Before moving boxes in, do one last walk-through with your landlord to document the apartment's condition.
