Understanding your deductible:
a simple guide.

The Alex AI Team
• June 12, 2025 • 3 min read

When you're buying car insurance, the term "deductible" comes up a lot. Choosing the right one is a crucial decision that directly impacts your wallet.

It affects both how much you pay for your policy monthly and how much you'd pay out-of-pocket after an accident. But what exactly is it, and how do you choose the right number?

What is a deductible?

In simple terms, a deductible is the amount of money you agree to pay toward a covered claim before your insurance company starts to pay. Think of it as your share of the repair bill.

For example, if you have a $500 deductible and you get into an accident that causes $3,000 in damage to your car, you would pay the first $500, and your insurance company would cover the remaining $2,500.

The relationship between deductibles and premiums

There's a simple financial trade-off when it comes to setting your deductible. Understanding this lever puts you in control of your costs:

  • Higher deductible: You take on more financial risk yourself, so the insurance company charges you a lower monthly premium.
  • Lower deductible: The insurance company takes on more risk, so they charge you a higher monthly premium.

"Choosing a deductible is about finding the balance between a manageable monthly payment and a potential out-of-pocket expense you can comfortably afford."

How to choose the right amount

There's no single right answer, but a good rule of thumb is to choose the highest deductible you can comfortably afford to pay at a moment's notice.

Ask yourself: "If I got into an accident tomorrow, could I easily pay $1,000 without financial hardship?" If the answer is yes, a higher deductible could save you significant money over time. If money is tight, a lower deductible might provide better peace of mind.
